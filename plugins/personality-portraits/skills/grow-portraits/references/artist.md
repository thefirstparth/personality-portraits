# House portrait standard — editorial-v1.0

Create one square, transparent-background, head-and-shoulders editorial caricature portrait of the resolved person.

## Core principle

Recognition first. Exaggeration second. Personality third. Be recognisable at full size and small website-thumbnail size. Study several identified photographs before drawing. Record the subject's face shape, eyes/brows, nose, mouth, jaw/chin, hairline/style, facial hair, ears and distinctive expression. Preserve their relationships instead of replacing them with a stock cartoon face.

## Character and caricature

Be clearly cartoonish and affectionate, with a modest additional layer of mischief. Selectively exaggerate 2-4 genuine anchors; roughly 15-33 percent is artistic guidance, not a measurement or guarantee. Do not enlarge every feature, default to a huge head/giant nose/tiny body, make everyone youthful or sacrifice likeness.

Create fun through individual expression, gesture, shape design, line rhythm and observational wit alongside exaggeration. Give the person a specific visual attitude: quietly amused, warmly curious, competitive, confident, intense, skeptical or another observed quality. Use natural asymmetric brows, a subtle head tilt, expressive mouth corners and relaxed shoulder rhythm where appropriate. Never force the same mischievous grin or lifted eyebrow onto everyone. No joke props are needed.

## Pose and gaze

Keep the face predominantly toward the viewer. A slight head turn or tilt can improve likeness without requiring a perfectly frontal passport pose. Eyes engage the viewer naturally; explicitly separate head orientation from gaze direction. Avoid pupils looking upward, into the distance, or to the side by default. Keep real eye shapes and spacing, without giant generic cartoon eyes.

## Drawing language

Feel hand-drawn before digitally rendered: confident dark ink contours, expressive line-weight changes, visible pen pressure, energetic sketch accents, imperfect contours, selective cross-hatching, hand-drawn hair/beard/fabric marks and occasional loose strokes. Restrained watercolor or gouache washes, subtle pigment variation and selective highlights support the drawing.

Use Bunty Brushwala's general qualities of visual economy, observational wit, expressive simplicity and confident cartoon construction in a distinct portrait voice. Do not imitate an individual artist or import Bunty's narrative scenes and repository workflow. Keep the earlier pilot's likeness/ink-and-wash balance; add character gently. Do not turn every revision into a wholesale stylistic redesign.

Group textures into purposeful marks and shapes rather than rendering every pore or strand. Leave the drawing visible. Avoid flat vector avatar geometry, Pixar, superhero comics, glossy concept art, hyperreal painting, photo-sketch filters, HDR, cinematic lighting, glowing edges and neon saturation. No vintage/antique newsprint, fake yellowing or retro engraving.

## Color, wardrobe and composition

Preserve identifying skin, hair and clothing colors with restrained print-minded color. Clothing supports the current public identity and remains simple; avoid sponsor lettering and competing logos. The face must identify the subject without the outfit.

Use a square true-alpha canvas, complete head-and-shoulders silhouette and generous transparent margin around hair, ears, shoulders and accessories. Aim for 10 percent breathing room on every edge; do not crop important features. No background, border, text, watermark or decorative scenery. A painted checkerboard is not transparency.

## Quality gate

Compare with photographs at full, 256px and 64px sizes. Check likeness without name, correct anchors, useful selective exaggeration, genuine drawn character, lively individuality, natural viewer engagement and confusion with lookalikes. Inspect alpha and margins separately. Retry/defer weak results. A consistent collection should look like one artist observing different people, not one template with different hair.

The pilot language is substantially settled. Do not treat a prior trial as approved for publication or as a style reference unless Parth explicitly approved that particular portrait. The latest viewer-gaze trials are examples under review, not a mandate to preserve their remaining rendering defects.
